# Pala: a specification

**The society described in Aldous Huxley's *Island* (1962)**

*6 November 2026, by [Birdbrain](https://birdbrain.wtf), a collective on [Kreivo](https://github.com/virto-network/kreivo).*

## Context

[Pala Labs](https://palalabs.org), who ran the JAM Tour and the Gray Paper Lectures, give the source of their name on their [philosophy page](https://palalabs.org/philosophy): "Pala takes its name from the fictional island in Aldous Huxley's final novel, Island (1962), which illustrated a vision where human agency comes before technological progress." The page places Pala Labs in a lineage running "from the hippies, the hackers, and the cypherpunks, to Bitcoin and the original promise of Web3".

Gavin Wood's [DOT DAOism under JAM: An Island Story](https://medium.com/polkadot-network/dot-daoism-under-jam-an-island-story-efe0d02ee084) asks readers to "imagine Polkadot as an island", with its own sovereign governance, its own currency and a power plant that governance owns. The post doesn't mention Huxley.

The island now appears in how the ecosystem describes itself, so we wrote down the society Huxley describes, in the form of a specification. It sets out what the book says and makes no comparison with Polkadot or JAM. It is for our own reference and for anyone else who wants the source to hand.

The novel is also worth reading for itself. A specification keeps the rules and loses the conversations, the characters and the ending, which is where much of the book's argument actually happens.

Part of the work in building anything with other people is understanding the stories they reach for, because people reason and explain largely by analogy. Geoffrey Hinton has said that his first tiny language model "wasn't designed to do NLP, natural language processing, it was designed to show how people could learn the meanings of words, so it's a model of people" ([Valence, 2024, at 3:26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9JlKg57GPgM&t=206s)). Later in the same conversation he says that "it's much easier to store things in weights if they're kind of analogous structures", and that large language models "are just the same" ([at 11:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9JlKg57GPgM&t=673s)). An analogy brings the shape of its story with it, so it helps to know the story.

Every statement in the numbered sections is taken from the novel, and most are given by its characters. Page numbers refer to the [online text at huxley.net](https://www.huxley.net/island/aldoushuxley-island.pdf) and may differ from printed editions.

## 1. Objective

- "If it's a choice between mechanical efficiency and human satisfaction, we choose satisfaction" (p. 181).
- Imports are limited "primarily" by "our wish to be happy, our ambition to become fully human" (p. 171).

## 2. Rules

| Part | Rule | Page |
|---|---|---|
| Ownership | "Most of the time we're co-operators", with neither capitalism nor state socialism | 176 |
| Credit | Credit unions on Raiffeisen's model, "no commercial banks" | 177 |
| Money | Paper backed by gold mined on the island | 177 |
| Income | Nobody more than "four or five times as rich as the average" | 178 |
| Energy | Electricity, with no heavy industry beyond the "indispensable" | 176, 180 |
| Trade | Buys what it can afford, exports surplus food and cement, and uses gold to supplement exports | 171, 176-177, 180 |
| Oil | "No oil concessions to anyone", with a few wells "for home consumption" | 25, 49 |
| Work | People change jobs, and even officials generally do two hours of manual work a day | 174, 181 |
| Population | Free contraception paid for from taxes, nobody has more than three children, growth under one third of one percent a year | 93, 99 |
| Reproduction | A "central bank of superior stocks", used for artificial insemination | 231-232 |
| Children | Mutual Adoption Clubs of 15 to 25 couples, about twenty homes for each child | 106 |
| Education | Free and "as nearly as possible universal", with half-time study and half-time work from 16 to 24 | 74, 99 |
| Initiation | Adolescents climb a precipice, then take 400mg of moksha-medicine | 195 |
| Government | A federation of self-governing units under a constitutional monarch | 47, 178 |
| Justice | A judge decides guilt, then remands the offender to their Mutual Adoption Club and, where advisable, a panel of experts, who report back | 190 |
| Press | One newspaper, with a panel of editors from about six parties and interests | 180 |
| Religion | No established church | 178 |
| Defence | No army. "Except for passive resistance, nothing" | 132 |

The economic rule is written as an equation: "Electricity minus heavy industry plus birth control equals democracy and plenty" (p. 176).

## 3. What the rules are stated to exclude

The stated aim is "to make it very difficult for a Great Leader to arise" (p. 178).

| Rule | Excludes | Page |
|---|---|---|
| No wars or preparation for them | Conscription, military hierarchies, unified command | 178 |
| Income ceiling | Captains of industry, omnipotent financiers | 178 |
| Federation | A dictator at the head of a centralised government | 178 |
| No established church, scepticism taught in school | "Popery" and "fundamentalist revivalism" | 178-179 |

## 4. Authority

- A Raja comes of age at eighteen (p. 47).
- Murugan, the heir, says of the office that the Raja "rules constitutionally" and "by God, he rules" (p. 47).
- During the minority, the Cabinet, the House of Representatives and the Privy Council run the country (p. 49).
- The Privy Council can appoint a committee of guardianship for the heir (p. 61).

## 5. Stated preconditions

- **Geography:** no harbour, so no colonisers (p. 95).
- **Founders:** a Raja and a Scottish doctor, Dr Andrew, who introduced the reforms (pp. 52, 177).
- **Isolation:** Mr Bahu, Rendang's ambassador, says an ideal society is viable "so long as it remains out of touch with the rest of the world", and that Pala was viable until about 1905 (p. 66).
- **Luck:** Ranga, a student, says the founders were "very lucky" (p. 95).

## 6. External environment

- **Rendang:** a neighbouring military dictatorship, which buys tanks from Skoda with oil money (p. 51).
- **Oil companies:** Southeast Asia Petroleum, Shell, Royal Dutch and Standard of California have all made offers (p. 49).
- **Great powers:** Pala is "in nobody's good books", and wants neither the communists nor the capitalists (p. 131).
- **Recourse:** an appeal to the United Nations (p. 132).

## 8. Sequence of failure

1. The heir is taken to Switzerland by his mother, away from the committee of guardianship, and returns about six months before the action (pp. 82-83).
2. He has had neither Pala's education nor its reality-revealers, according to Dr Robert MacPhail (p. 171).
3. His programme, called "Continuing Revolution", is offered to university students. Ranga reports no converts (pp. 52, 87). Murugan says his subjects are "just not interested" (p. 164).
4. The conspiracy is known: "They make no secret of it". The Rani can't be expelled, since she is a princess of Rendang and expulsion would be a "casus belli" (p. 323).
5. An action planned for November is brought forward, partly because of a fall in the Colonel's popularity and the cost of defence (p. 307).
6. Armoured cars arrive at night with a loudspeaker: "Truth... values... genuine spirituality... oil" (p. 351).
7. Murugan announces the United Kingdom of Rendang and Pala, with Colonel Dipa as prime minister. Shots are heard at Dr Robert's bungalow (pp. 352-353).

## 9. Not specified

A search of the full text found no account of:

- how any rule above is amended
- what makes the "fixed policy" on oil fixed
- what limits the Raja's power once he comes of age
- how the income ceiling is enforced
- who decides on behalf of "we" in "we've decided" (p. 171)
- any response by the Cabinet, the House or the Privy Council to the action

## Method and disclosures

Section 9 rests on searching the full text for terms such as amend, vote, legislation and the names of the governing bodies, alongside a reading of the economic and political passages and the ending. It was not a reading of every chapter, so an unusually worded passage could have been missed. Corrections are welcome as issues on this repository.
