# Pala: a specification

**The society described in Aldous Huxley's *Island* (1962)**

*6 November 2026, by [Birdbrain](https://birdbrain.wtf), a collective on [Kreivo](https://github.com/virto-network/kreivo).*

## Context

[Pala Labs](https://palalabs.org), who ran the JAM Tour and the Gray Paper Lectures, give the source of their name on their [philosophy page](https://palalabs.org/philosophy): "Pala takes its name from the fictional island in Aldous Huxley's final novel, Island (1962), which illustrated a vision where human agency comes before technological progress." The page places Pala Labs in a lineage running "from the hippies, the hackers, and the cypherpunks, to Bitcoin and the original promise of Web3".

Gavin Wood's [DOT DAOism under JAM: An Island Story](https://medium.com/polkadot-network/dot-daoism-under-jam-an-island-story-efe0d02ee084) asks readers to "imagine Polkadot as an island", with its own sovereign governance, its own currency and a power plant that governance owns. The post doesn't mention Huxley.

The island now appears in how the ecosystem describes itself, so we wrote down the society Huxley describes, in the form of a specification. It sets out what the book says and makes no comparison with Polkadot or JAM. It is for our own reference and for anyone else who wants the source to hand.

The novel is also worth reading for itself. A specification keeps the rules and loses the conversations, the characters and the ending, which is where much of the book's argument actually happens.

Part of the work in building anything with other people is understanding the stories they reach for, because people reason and explain largely by analogy. Geoffrey Hinton has said that his first tiny language model "wasn't designed to do NLP, natural language processing, it was designed to show how people could learn the meanings of words, so it's a model of people" ([Valence, 2024, at 3:26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9JlKg57GPgM&t=206s)). Later in the same conversation he says that "it's much easier to store things in weights if they're kind of analogous structures", and that large language models "are just the same" ([at 11:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9JlKg57GPgM&t=673s)). An analogy brings the shape of its story with it, so it helps to know the story.

Every statement in the numbered sections is taken from the novel, and most are given by its characters. Where a rule is stated only by an outsider or by an opponent of Pala's arrangements, the speaker is named. Page numbers refer to the [online text at huxley.net](https://www.huxley.net/island/aldoushuxley-island.pdf) and may differ from printed editions.

## 1. Objective

- "If it's a choice between mechanical efficiency and human satisfaction, we choose satisfaction" (p. 181).
- Imports are limited "primarily" by "our wish to be happy, our ambition to become fully human" (p. 171).

## 2. Rules

| Part | Rule | Page |
|---|---|---|
| Ownership | "Most of the time we're co-operators", with neither capitalism nor state socialism | 176 |
| Credit | Credit unions on Raiffeisen's model, "no commercial banks in your Western style" | 177 |
| Money | Paper backed by gold mined on the island, with coins of silver, gold and copper | 177 |
| Income | Nobody more than "four or five times as rich as the average" | 178 |
| Energy | Electricity, and no heavy industry. Cement is "one of the indispensable industries", electrical equipment one of the "indispensable imports" | 176, 180 |
| Trade | Imports only what it can't make and can afford. Exports surplus food and cement, and uses gold to supplement exports | 171, 176-177, 180 |
| Oil | Refused. "No oil concessions to anyone. It's their fixed policy", in the words of an oil company's chairman. A few wells "for home consumption". The latest offer was turned down "less than a month ago" | 25, 49, 132 |
| Entry | A "forbidden island" from the founding, closed to "missionaries, planters and traders". Visitors are graded, with armament salesmen, missionaries, oilmen and uranium prospectors as "the Alpha Plus undesirables" | 133, 157 |
| Work | People change jobs. Even officials generally do two hours of manual work a day, and children start at fourteen with ninety minutes | 174, 181 |
| Health | Doctors are "paid for keeping people well". The old get pensions if they can't earn | 78, 107 |
| Population | Contraceptives free and tax-supported, "though not compulsory, as nearly as possible universal". Growth under a third of one percent a year. "Nobody has more than three, and most people stop at two" is reported as an outcome | 93, 99 |
| Reproduction | A "central bank of superior stocks" for artificial insemination, which "practically all the couples who decide to have a third child" use | 231-232 |
| Children | Mutual Adoption Clubs of 15 to 25 couples, about twenty homes for each child, run "by its members", not the government. A child may move to another home | 106, 108 |
| Education | Half-time study and half-time work from 16 to 24. Scepticism is part of the curriculum, and so is climbing | 74, 178, 191 |
| Initiation | Passing "out of childhood into adolescence", children climb a precipice and are then given 400mg of the moksha-medicine | 195 |
| Science | The emphasis is on "the sciences of life". Physics and chemistry are taken from abroad | 259 |
| Government | A constitutional monarchy, and "a federation of self-governing units, geographical units, professional units, economic units" | 47, 178 |
| Justice | A judge decides guilt, then remands the offender to their Mutual Adoption Club and, where advisable, a local panel of medical and "mycomystical" experts, who report back. Most crimes are dealt with by the club alone | 190 |
| Press | One newspaper, with a panel of editors from "half a dozen different parties and interests" | 180 |
| Religion | No established church. Popular religion is neither encouraged nor discouraged | 178, 220 |
| Defence | No army and no firearms. "Except for passive resistance, nothing" | 132, 351 |

The economic rule is written as an equation: "Electricity minus heavy industry plus birth control equals democracy and plenty. Electricity plus heavy industry minus birth control equals misery, totalitarianism and war" (p. 176).

## 3. What the rules are stated to exclude

The stated aim is "to make it very difficult for a Great Leader to arise" (p. 178).

| Rule | Excludes | Page |
|---|---|---|
| No wars or preparation for them | Conscription, military hierarchies, unified command | 178 |
| Income ceiling | Captains of industry, omnipotent financiers | 178 |
| Federation | "Omnipotent politicians or bureaucrats", and a dictator at the head of a centralised government | 178 |
| No established church, scepticism taught in school | "Popery" and "fundamentalist revivalism", and the "eloquent rabble-rouser" | 178-179 |

Alongside the rules, the book describes preventive measures applied to people:

- **Screening:** every child is examined between four and a half and five, and those likely to bully get "appropriate treatment" at once (p. 185).
- **Deflection:** the love of power is turned "away from people and on to things", through forestry, mining and the sea (p. 188). Climbing is "another preventive substitute for bullying" (p. 196).
- **Suggestibility:** the most hypnotisable children are "trained not to be hypnotizable by the enemies of liberty" (p. 251).

Vijaya, a Palanese, says that "our political arrangements make it practically impossible for them to domineer on any larger scale" (p. 188). The arrangements themselves aren't described.

## 4. Authority

- The throne is hereditary (p. 40). Murugan has been Raja since his father died in 1954, and is his only child (pp. 46-47).
- A Raja comes of age at eighteen (p. 47). Murugan's coming of age is "next week" when the action begins (p. 279), and Colonel Dipa is to attend on a state visit (p. 132).
- Murugan says of the office that the Raja "rules constitutionally" and "by God, he rules" (p. 47).
- During the minority, "three sets of old fogeys" govern, in Murugan's words: the Cabinet, the House of Representatives, and the Privy Council "representing me, the Raja" (p. 49). The Privy Council meets at Shivapuram, and Dr Robert MacPhail attends it (p. 39).
- The Privy Council appointed a committee of guardianship for Murugan, according to the Rani (p. 61).
- There is one precedent from the founding: a Raja "dissolved the Council of Regency and resumed his royal powers", with Dr Andrew as his chief adviser (p. 154).
- Parties exist, since the newspaper's editors represent them (p. 180). How office is filled is not described.

## 5. Stated preconditions

- **Geography:** no harbour, so no colonisers (pp. 95-96).
- **Founders:** the Raja of the Reform and a Scottish doctor, Dr Andrew MacPhail. "Between them, he and my great-grandfather invented modern Pala. The Old Raja consolidated their work" (Dr Robert, p. 40). The Old Raja's *Notes on What's What* "merely states the underlying principles" (p. 40).
- **Isolation:** Mr Bahu, Rendang's ambassador, says an ideal society is viable "so long as it remains out of touch with the rest of the world", and that Pala was viable until about 1905 (p. 66).
- **Luck:** Ranga, a student, says the founders were "very lucky" (p. 95).
- **Order:** "Public health and social reform are the indispensable preconditions of any kind of general enlightenment" (Susila MacPhail, p. 293).
- **Behaviour:** the society "will remain nice only if everybody works and behaves decently" (p. 236).

## 6. External environment

- **Rendang:** a neighbouring military dictatorship under Colonel Dipa (pp. 21, 24). Its population grows by almost three percent a year (p. 93). It has bought tanks and guns from Skoda (p. 94) and has a further sixty-million-dollar deal with the Czechs (p. 130). Dipa's "Greater Rendang" speeches claim "the whole of Pala" (p. 131).
- **The Rani:** Murugan's mother is a princess of Rendang, daughter of the late Sultan (p. 58). Bahu stays with her (p. 44).
- **Oil companies:** Southeast Asia Petroleum, Shell, Royal Dutch and Standard of California have all made offers, according to Murugan (p. 49).
- **Great powers:** Pala is "in nobody's good books", and wants neither the communists nor the capitalists. The powers may prefer "a Rendang-controlled Pala with oil fields to an independent Pala without" (p. 131).
- **Recourse:** an appeal to the United Nations, and remonstrance through "our minister in Rendang-Lobo" (p. 132).

## 8. Sequence of failure

1. Lord Aldehyde, an oil company's chairman, sends Will to "find out if there's a pro-oil minority and ask how we could help them" (p. 25).
2. The Rani left Murugan on Pala under the guardianship committee while she travelled (p. 61). She then took him to Switzerland, by her account for his health (p. 63). They came back about six months before Will arrives, and spent half that time in Rendang (pp. 82-83). He belongs to no Mutual Adoption Club (p. 310), and according to Dr Robert has had neither Pala's education nor its reality-revealers (p. 171).
3. Murugan meets Colonel Dipa in secret (p. 45). He calls his programme "Continuing Revolution" (p. 52). Three weeks before the story it is put to a group of university students, and Ranga reports no converts (p. 87).
4. Representatives of three oil companies fly into Rendang-Lobo, according to the Rani. A coded cable goes to Lord Aldehyde through the Rendang Legation in London, asking him to empower Will to act (pp. 214-215).
5. The conspiracy is known: "They make no secret of it". Murugan and the Rani can't be expelled, because the Rani is a princess of Rendang and expulsion would be a "casus belli". The stated response is to "try to keep them in order, try to change their minds, hope for a happy outcome, and be prepared for the worst" (p. 323).
6. An action planned for the night of a lunar eclipse in November is brought forward, for reasons the Rani gives as "partly political", "partly Economic (the burdens of Defense are too onerous to be borne by Rendang alone) and partly Astrological" (p. 307).
7. Before dawn, after distant gunfire, vehicles arrive with a loudspeaker. "Your Raja speaking" asks Palanese to welcome "your friends from across the Strait", and then the loudspeaker sounds: "Truth... values... genuine spirituality... oil" (pp. 350-351).
8. Murugan announces the United Kingdom of Rendang and Pala, with Colonel Dipa as its first prime minister (p. 352). The vehicles stop at Dr Robert's bungalow, and a single shot is heard, then a burst from an automatic rifle (pp. 352-353). The book ends on the next page.

## 9. Not specified

On a full reading, the book gives no account of the following. Where the text comes close, it is noted.

- **How a rule is amended.** The founding reforms were decided by the Raja of the Reform and Dr Andrew (pp. 99, 105-106, 157). Murugan means "to reform these so-called reforms" (p. 64). No procedure is given for either.
- **What makes the "fixed policy" on oil fixed.** It is attributed to "the Palanese government" (p. 65), and during the minority to bodies that "won't allow anyone else to do anything about it" (p. 49). Reasons are given (pp. 131, 323). No mechanism is.
- **What limits the Raja once he comes of age.** The monarchy is "constitutional" (p. 47), and Vijaya says the political arrangements prevent domination (p. 188). Neither passage says how.
- **How the income ceiling is enforced.** It is attributed to "our economic system" (p. 178).
- **Who decides on behalf of "we".** "We've decided" is used of scooters (p. 171), and Gobind Singh's paintings aren't exported "because we like his work too well" (p. 222). Dr Robert grants Will a month's stay on his own responsibility (p. 134), and Susila speaks for "we" on deportation without holding a stated office (p. 323). Mutual Adoption Clubs are "run by its members" (p. 108). No body or procedure is named for national decisions.
- **Any response by the Cabinet, the House or the Privy Council to the action.** None is described before the book ends on p. 354.
