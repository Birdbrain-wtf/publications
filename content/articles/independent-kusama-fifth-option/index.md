# An independent Kusama on a reduced JAM, keeping its validators

**The fifth option**

Birdbrain · for discussion · 20 October 2026

## Who is writing, and why

We write as stakeholders in Kusama, and for the part of it that has no line on the ballot as it stands:

- **Independent validators**, who secure Kusama today and would stop under all four options.
- **Parachains other than the system chains**, including Kreivo, where our members hold their membership.
- **Builders on Kusama Asset Hub** whose applications account for more than a quarter of its active addresses.
- **dUSD**, the only US-dollar stablecoin still issued natively on Kusama. Tether stopped issuing USDt on Kusama in August 2023 and froze what remained on 1 September 2025. Brale issues it, with Decent Partners as its programme partner.

This group has come to OpenGov in good faith, through treasury proposals and bounties, and has put its own time and money into extending what Kusama can do. It has done so without engagement or support from the bodies that wrote the four options, would fund them or would maintain the result. Our interests in full are in the Disclosures at the end.

## Summary

On 20 October 2026 four referenda were submitted to Kusama's Wish for Change track, [#671](https://kusama.subsquare.io/referenda/671), [#672](https://kusama.subsquare.io/referenda/672), [#673](https://kusama.subsquare.io/referenda/673) and [#674](https://kusama.subsquare.io/referenda/674). Each asks KSM holders to move Kusama onto Polkadot's JAM network, "JAM Prime", for a fixed number of cores over a fixed number of years. They differ in the number of cores and the length of the term.

All four end Kusama's own validator set and retire staking.

KSM holders have already voted on this question. [Referendum #573](https://kusama.subsquare.io/referenda/573), "Kusama JAM Upgrade: Option A - Lightweight and independent", passed on 21 August 2025. It asked for a smaller Kusama running its own JAM network, with its own validators and KSM as its native token. Each of the four new referenda says it would supersede #573 if selected. None puts #573 itself on the ballot beside them.

We are proposing a fifth referendum, [#676](https://kusama.subsquare.io/referenda/676), on the same track and in the same voting window, that continues #573: **an independent Kusama, keeping its own validators on a reduced JAM, is scoped, priced and specified, and debated beside JAM Prime before Kusama's validators are retired.**

The four options come with a price. Independence has never been given one. Nobody has asked Kusama's validators what it costs them to run a seat, and nobody has written down what a reduced JAM of Kusama's own would need. The fifth option asks for that work to be done in the open, with the people who run the network, so KSM holders can compare two priced directions instead of one priced direction and a blank.

It asks nothing from the Web3 Foundation, from DOT DAO or from the Kusama Vision fund.

It protects the people who run and secure Kusama today while the comparison is made. And it comes with a steward: Birdbrain offers to form an open collective that takes on the Kusama-specific work the four options would give to the Polkadot Technical Fellowship.

The four options also leave open who would hold Kusama's side of the deal. The promise would sit with DOT DAO, which is being asked this week to put its own product first, and whose legal arm has no [directors elected by DOT holders](https://forum.polkadot.network/t/appointment-of-dot-directors/16006/10?u=birdbrain), let alone KSM holders.

An independent Kusama is the one line whose protections KSM holders would enforce themselves. That is why it needs to be a credible, priced offer and not a protest vote.

A steward needs legal standing of its own, or it is only as accountable as whoever signs for it. Birdbrain is giving itself that standing onshore and in its members' hands: a Wyoming nonprofit association, first as a plain Unincorporated Nonprofit Association and then as a Decentralized one (a DUNA) once it has the hundred members the law requires. Its members would be its voters. It is the opposite shape to the PCF, a Cayman foundation run by directors.

The fifth option is also the start of something larger. Once Birdbrain's membership is a credible association of infrastructure partners with a fully costed plan, KSM holders who want a different path can delegate their votes to it, and by stake-weighted vote that path could be enacted, keeping Kusama's chaos going.

We are not asking anyone to vote against JAM Prime.

We are asking for independence to be on the ballot, so that if KSM holders choose to reverse #573, they do it by comparing the two, not by never being asked.

## 1. Background for new readers

**Kusama** is a public blockchain network, launched in 2019 as the faster, rougher sibling of Polkadot. It has its own token, KSM, its own validators (the machines that produce and check blocks, currently 700) and its own on-chain governance, in which KSM holders vote on referenda. Parachains are its main customers, consuming coretime.

One of them, Kusama Asset Hub, holds most of the network's tokens and applications. Another, Kreivo, gives groups of people on-chain membership with a passkey.

**JAM** (the "Join-Accumulate Machine") is the next generation of Polkadot's technology, specified in the [Gray Paper](https://graypaper.com). It replaces the current relay chain with a more general computer that sells processing time in units called **cores**. A JAM network can be large or small.

**Wish for Change** is a Kusama governance track for decisions of intent. A Wish for Change referendum moves no funds and changes no code. It records what KSM holders want, and the code and funding follow in later referenda.

**Three referenda matter here:**

| Referendum | What it said | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [#573](https://kusama.subsquare.io/referenda/573), Wish for Change | Kusama becomes "lightweight and independent" on JAM: 32 cores, one-second blocks, KSM as the native token for staking, storage and coretime, inflation estimated at about 2.4% a year at worst | Passed, confirmed 21 August 2025 |
| [#655](https://kusama.subsquare.io/referenda/655) | First step towards #573: Kusama's validator set reduced from 1,000 to 700 | Passed and executed |
| #671 to #674, Wish for Change | Kusama moves onto JAM Prime, Polkadot's network, for 8, 6, 4 or 2 cores, over 5, 6.7, 10 or 20 years | Submitted 20 October 2026, deciding from the same day, closing about 17 November |

## 2. The four options, briefly

The four referenda come from [Kusama on JAM Prime: the infrastructure decision](https://forum.polkadot.network/t/kusama-on-jam-prime-the-infrastructure-decision/19044), the third paper in a series that began in September. The design is the same in all four:

- **Kusama's validators stop.** Kusama's security comes from Polkadot's validators instead. Staking on Kusama is retired when the move completes.
- **Kusama pays for its place in DOT.** Each option is priced at US$108,000 a core-year, US$4.32m in total, paid in DOT from the Web3 Foundation's Kusama Vision fund and capped at 5 million DOT. That fund is the 10 million DOT that KSM holders accepted in [#498](https://kusama.subsquare.io/referenda/498) in March 2025, to be used "for the sole benefit of Kusama", in the same vote that recognised Kusama as "an experiment-centric peer network of Polkadot" rather than its canary.
- **The Polkadot Technical Fellowship maintains it.** Kusama's system chains (Asset Hub, Coretime, People, Bridge Hub and Encointer) run inside the allocation. The options with fewer cores need an operating arrangement that has not yet been designed.
- **The deal is made with DOT DAO**, Polkadot's governance, and is protected against "ordinary unilateral revocation".

The paper argues that sharing infrastructure is cheaper than running it alone, measured against a benchmark of US$3,000 per validator per month. That benchmark has not been tested against what Kusama's own operators spend, and the cost of independence has not been worked out at all. A comparison with one side priced is not yet a comparison.

## 3. Why a fifth option belongs on the ballot

**A passed Wish for Change is the only standing instruction KSM holders have given.** #573 passed, and #655 carried out its first step. Each of the four says it would supersede #573's direction "upon selection". None asks KSM holders to choose between #573 and JAM Prime. The round chooses among four versions of JAM Prime, and #573 falls as a side effect of whichever wins.

**The rule that sets #573 aside also sets aside whatever replaces it.** A Wish for Change carries weight because the next proposer treats it as settled until KSM holders say otherwise. If a passed wish can be set aside by a clause in different ones, without ever being put to the vote itself, then whichever of #671 to #674 wins carries no more weight than #573 did, and the next proposer can do the same to it. The way to keep the track meaningful is to put the existing instruction on the ballot beside the alternatives and let the vote decide between them.

**Four options that share a premise are one choice.** #671 to #674 differ in cores and years. They agree on everything else: Kusama's validators stop, staking ends, Kusama's security and maintenance are bought from Polkadot. A voter who wants Kusama to keep its own validators has no line to vote for. They can vote no four times, but four noes record a refusal, not a preference.

**The people most affected have the least to gain from the four.** Validators lose their role. Nominators and liquid-staking holders lose their yield together, with no published unbonding schedule. Parachains other than the system chains lose guaranteed cores and have to buy processing time in DOT while earning in KSM. A fifth line lets them say what they want, not only what they refuse.

**One procedural point.** On the Wish for Change track, the four options are counted separately and each early approval fixes its tally. The fifth sits outside that selection rule, because it is not a fifth version of JAM Prime. It can pass alongside any of the four. We ask that it is decided in the same voting window, on the same track and thresholds, and that the results are published side by side.

### Who would hold the other side of the deal

The four options make Kusama's security, maintenance and protected rights a promise from DOT DAO. Their paper does not say which body would hold that promise, and DOT DAO is not a legal person. Two things happening on Polkadot this week bear on how long such a promise would last.

**DOT DAO's direction is being set now.** [Referendum 1954](https://polkadot.subsquare.io/referenda/1954), the DOT DAO Strategy Mandate, is deciding. It asks DOT DAO to "avoid fragmented tools or efforts that don't drive user adoption for the Polkadot Product Platform", and invites the Technical Fellowship to treat that strategy as "a core evaluation criterion during specification reviews" ([proposal](https://forum.polkadot.network/t/a-unified-strategy-for-polkadot-alignment-on-value-capturing-goals-for-polkadot-s-product-platform-and-the-dotdao/18999)). Maintaining Kusama for up to twenty years does not bring users to the Polkadot Product Platform. If 1954 passes, the DAO asked to accept the Kusama package, and the Fellowship asked to maintain it, would both hold a standing instruction that ranks it below the DAO's own product. On 20 October, referendum 1954 had about 421,600 DOT of support, about 0.03% of issued DOT.

**Its legal arm has no directors chosen by DOT holders.** Where Polkadot governance needs a legal person, to sign a contract or hold an account, it uses the Polkadot Community Foundation (PCF), a Cayman foundation set up by [referendum 730](https://polkadot.subsquare.io/referenda/730) to act on OpenGov's instructions. Its bylaws let the directors reject a token-holder-approved proposal addressed to the Foundation within sixty days, if they judge it would breach their duties to the Foundation, harm the Foundation (its reputation included) or is not adequately funded. The two seats DOT holders are meant to elect have never been filled: the referenda to set up and hold that election ([1737](https://polkadot.subsquare.io/referenda/1737), [1749](https://polkadot.subsquare.io/referenda/1749)) were both rejected in October 2025, and we found no published transparency report. The forum thread on [appointing those directors](https://forum.polkadot.network/t/appointment-of-dot-directors/16006/10) was reopened on 20 October because of 1954. Whether any part of the Kusama package would pass through the PCF is not said.

None of this suggests anyone means to break a promise to Kusama. It means the promise would sit with a DAO being asked to narrow its priorities, whose legal arm answers to a board that DOT holders do not elect, and KSM holders have no say in at all. The four options protect Kusama against "ordinary unilateral revocation". Nobody has said who decides what counts as ordinary. That is why an independent Kusama has to be a credible, priced offer: it is the one line on the ballot whose protections KSM holders would enforce themselves.

### Why legal standing matters, and the shape we are choosing

A network's governance can vote, but it cannot sign a contract, hold a bank account, employ anyone or be sued. Something has to do those things for it, and whoever does holds real power over what the votes mean in practice. On Polkadot that is the PCF: a foundation in the Cayman Islands, offshore by design, where the directors have the last word on what the Foundation does and DOT holders have not filled the seats they are meant to elect.

Without any legal body, the alternative is worse. Some US courts have treated DAOs with no wrapper as general partnerships, which leaves every member personally liable for the group's debts. For a collective that makes anyone who taps a passkey a member, that is not a theoretical risk.

Wyoming offers a third shape, written for distributed networks. A **DUNA** (Decentralized Unincorporated Nonprofit Association, created by Wyoming law in 2024 and amended in 2026) is a legal entity with limited liability for its members, governed by its members, with no shares and no board of directors to overrule them. Its governing principles can include smart contracts and enacted on-chain proposals, so the votes members already cast become the rules the entity has to follow. Uniswap's governance adopted one in 2025. A DUNA needs at least one hundred members. Below that, the same Act provides a plain Unincorporated Nonprofit Association (UNA), with the same liability shield and legal personality, from two members.

**Where Birdbrain is.** We have published the case for it as [RFC 003](https://birdbrain.wtf/rfc/rfc-003-legal-wrapper-una-duna) and drafted the association's governing principles. The plan is a UNA first, electing into a DUNA once community 1786 passes one hundred members (it has 43 today). Nothing is filed until Birdbrain's members approve the association agreement by their own on-chain vote, and that vote is what forms the association. Its members are the members of community 1786, and they are its voters. The operator account that administers 1786 on Kreivo becomes the association's administrator, acting on their referenda.

**Why this matters for Kusama.** A steward of an independent Kusama would hold commitments, sign agreements with client teams and validators, receive treasury funds against milestones and answer for what it ships. Under this shape, the body doing that would be onshore, legally answerable, and governed by the people taking part, not by directors that neither KSM holders nor its own members choose. A wrapper is not a regulatory exemption, and we will take legal advice in the US and the UK before filing.

## 4. The fifth option

### What KSM holders would be voting for

> Kusama continues the direction set by Referendum #573. An independent Kusama, keeping its own validator set, staking, governance and monetary policy on a reduced JAM network of its own, is scoped, priced and specified in the open, with Kusama's validators, nominators and parachains, and put to KSM holders beside JAM Prime before any transition retires Kusama's validators. Kusama-specific maintenance is carried by an open steward collective accountable to KSM DAO. No funds are requested by this referendum.

That is the resolution in short. On chain it runs to six clauses, which set out the direction, its relation to #671 to #674, what the comparison needs, how validators, stakers and parachains are protected while it is made, the steward and the funding. If both this and one of the four pass, the comparison comes before DOT DAO is asked to accept a package.

What follows is the starting brief for that work: the principles the scoping would test, not a finished specification.

### The network

- **Reduced JAM, on #573's parameters.** 32 cores, KSM as the native token for staking, storage and coretime. Polkadot runs a much larger JAM network. Kusama runs a small one of its own, built from the same open-source software.
- **Staged, not switched.** Kusama keeps running today's relay chain until a JAM client can run a Kusama-sized network that has passed a public test. Each stage is its own referendum.
- **One-second blocks are a target, not a promise.** JAM's specification uses six-second slots. Whether #573's one-second blocks can be met is one of the first things the public test will answer, and we will say plainly if they cannot.

### The validators

- **Kusama keeps a validator set, elected by KSM stake as now.** Nominated staking, election and slashing continue, rewritten as a JAM service when the network moves.
- **Today's operators keep their seats through the transition.** The first JAM set is drawn from Kusama's elected validators at the time of the switch, by the same election as today. No outside party chooses who runs a seat.
- **The set is as large as the network can pay for, with a floor.** JAM checks each core's work with a group of three validators, so 32 cores need at least 96. The current 700 shrinks only in steps that KSM holders approve, as [#655](https://kusama.subsquare.io/referenda/655) did. How far it needs to shrink is what the costing in Section 5 has to answer.
- **Spread is measured, not assumed.** A public dashboard reports, every era, the share of seats by hosting provider and by country. Limits on concentration are set by KSM holders once they can see the numbers, not imposed on existing operators at the start.

### Stakers and nominators

- **Staking continues.** Nobody's stake is forced to unbond.
- **Issuance comes down on a published schedule**, towards the 2.4% a year #573 estimated, with each step tied to a measure: the network's running cost met by its commission pot, and revenue from use. If the measures are not met, the schedule pauses rather than cutting validators below what keeps the network running.

### Parachains and Asset Hub

- **Parachains keep a home on Kusama.** Coretime stays priced in KSM and sold by Kusama's own Coretime chain, so a parachain that earns in KSM pays in KSM.
- **Asset Hub stays a Kusama system chain** on the same cores budget, with nothing squeezed into an operating arrangement designed after the vote.
- **Nothing a team builds today has to wait.** Membership on Kreivo, records and payments on Asset Hub carry on as they are while the transition runs.

### Governance

Kusama's referenda keep deciding Kusama's runtime, treasury and issuance. No agreement with another network's governance is required, so there is no promise to enforce across two DAOs.

## 5. What it would cost, and how we find out

**This option asks for no DOT and no payment to another network.** Kusama is paid for today the way it would be paid for under this option: validators by the commission share of staking issuance, the steward collective by KSM DAO's treasury through ordinary proposals against milestones.

**What we do not know yet is the cost.** The four options are priced against a benchmark of US$3,000 per validator per month, taken from the [earlier JAM Prime paper](https://forum.polkadot.network/t/a-proposal-for-a-kusama-future-on-jam-prime/18683). We have our own estimates, and we are not putting them forward, because nobody has yet asked the people who run Kusama's seats what it costs them. A cost for independence should come from them.

What the commission pot pays each validator today can be read on chain. What it costs to run a seat cannot, and that is the gap the costing closes.

**How it gets priced.** The first months of the mandate produce three published things:

1. **A cost survey of Kusama's validators.** Hardware, hosting, bandwidth, labour and tooling, by line, from operators of different sizes and in different countries, published in aggregate so no single operator is exposed.
2. **A reference configuration for a Kusama-sized JAM network.** Cores, validator count, hardware and slot time, checked on a public test network run by Kusama validators.
3. **A funding model, set against JAM Prime's on the same terms.** What the commission pot covers at today's issuance and at #573's 2.4%, what has to come from revenue from use, and how far issuance can fall before validators have to be cut.

Each is published with its workings, for anyone to check or dispute, before any transition referendum is put up.

**Security is a separate question.** A cheaper validator set is not easier to attack. Security comes from the KSM staked behind the validators, and the hosting bill does not change that.

**Set side by side.** The four options cost US$4.32m in DOT up front, end all commission income on Kusama, and leave renewal at the end of the term to a later negotiation. This option costs Kusama nothing to vote for, and asks only that the alternative is priced before the choice is made.

### How we would approach the Kusama treasury

The fifth referendum asks for no funds. The steward's work would be paid for later, through ordinary treasury proposals, and we have already tried the first of them, so here is what we tried and what we learned.

**Our first ask: a bounty for memberships.** In September we put up [referendum #665](https://kusama.subsquare.io/referenda/665), "Bounty: fund memberships only for people the room can vouch for". It asked for **8 KSM** on the Small Tipper track, as a treasury bounty curated by Birdbrain, to pay for Kreivo memberships at about 0.3 KSM each. The money was the small part. What it tested was a spending rule a curator cannot bend. A membership was payable only if all four of these held, and anyone could check them:

1. The attendance record for the session was anchored on Kusama Asset Hub before any payment against it. Our first one is at [block #21,513,860](https://assethub-kusama.subscan.io/block/21513860).
2. The person was in that record, with a proof that recomputes.
3. Their account held a Birdbrain membership on Kreivo minted at or after the session.
4. That person had not already had a membership paid for by the bounty. One person, one claim, ever.

The checker is [published](https://github.com/Birdbrain-wtf/attendance-checker), so the curator's judgement shrinks to "did the checker pass", and any dispute is settled by running it again. The money could only go to Birdbrain's sovereign account on Asset Hub, which no single key controls. We said plainly that this is checkable, not trustless: the record of who was in the room is witnessed by the people who were there.

**#665 was rejected on 30 September**, with about 60 KSM for and 4,335 KSM against, counted with conviction. Eleven accounts voted, and the one objector we could identify gave no reason. We take two things from it. Even a small, rule-bound ask fails without voters who already know the people asking. And the treasury is right to want evidence before it pays for members.

**The case behind it: [RFC 001](https://birdbrain.wtf/rfc/rfc-001-treasury-as-onboarding-subsidy).** The bounty was the first step of a larger argument we published in July: that Kusama's treasury should spend KSM onboarding members of real collectives instead of selling it for dollars. Its numbers are a model, written to be argued with, and we give them as that, not as a forecast:

| RFC 001, July 2026 | Figure |
| --- | --- |
| Treasury at the time | about 838,464 KSM, 4.5% of supply |
| Cost of one membership on Kreivo | 0.3 KSM, plus a 0.5 KSM deposit shared by each collective, so 0.3 + 0.5/N for a collective of N |
| One member in a collective of 20 | 0.325 KSM, about US$1.35 at today's price |
| One million members, in collectives of 20 | about 325,000 KSM, roughly 39% of the treasury then, about US$1.35m today |
| Cost per member still active, if 40% stay | about 0.81 KSM |
| Cost per member still active, if 20% stay | about 1.6 KSM |
| Outside demand for KSM from a retained base, per year | about 5,300 KSM (conservative), 18,600 (base) or 51,900 (strong) |

The last row is the most speculative. It rests on how many subsidised members stay and what they do, which nobody has measured. RFC 001 also says plainly that KSM moved from the treasury into memberships is not new demand: it stays inside Kusama instead of being sold, and the outside demand has to come from what the members then do.

**How we would approach it from here.**

- **Small, rule-bound asks, each against a published milestone.** No envelope we can draw on at our own discretion. Each request is its own referendum, with the rule it is paid under and the checker that proves it.
- **Evidence first.** The first year's outputs in Section 7 (the cost survey, the test network, the specification) come before any request to fund the transition itself.
- **From the collective, to an account no one person controls.** Requests come from Birdbrain, and payments go to its sovereign account on Asset Hub, decided by its members.
- **With our open position in view.** Decent Partners treats the 9,706 KSM from referendum #474 as owed to the treasury (see Disclosures), and we expect any future request to be weighed with that in mind.

## 6. The steward: Birdbrain

The four options give Kusama's maintenance to the Polkadot Technical Fellowship. That is a reasonable choice for a network inside Polkadot's. An independent Kusama needs a steward accountable to KSM holders.

**Birdbrain offers to form that steward as an open collective**, and to take on the Kusama-specific work:

- Kusama runtime releases and parameter changes, proposed to KSM DAO.
- The genesis and configuration of Kusama's own JAM network, and the public test before it.
- The staged transition plan, each stage its own referendum.
- The validator and spread dashboard, published every era.
- A quarterly report to KSM DAO: what shipped, what did not, and what each figure read on chain.

**Open by design.** Anyone who joins with their own passkey becomes a member on Kreivo, and members decide the collective's spending by referendum, one member, one vote. Those same members would be the voters of Birdbrain's Wyoming association, which gives the steward a legal body of its own (Section 3).

Validators, nominators, parachain teams and JAM client teams are invited to join and carry the work with us. The steward reuses the open Polkadot SDK and JAM software. It maintains Kusama's configuration of that software and does not fork the protocol.

**Where Birdbrain started.** Birdbrain was first put to KSM holders in March 2025, five days after #498 was enacted, as Wish for Change [#506](https://kusama.subsquare.io/referenda/506): "Establish Birdbrain as Kusama's on-chain innovation agency". It asked for a leaderless collective on Kreivo to help coordinate part of the 10 million DOT towards a Kusama that stood as a peer network, under the idea "create chaOS". Its principles were that the collective earns real income, treats any treasury funding as an advance repaid to the treasury, runs on lean governance, has no fixed leaders and shares value openly among its members. It also aimed to be a template other groups could adopt. #506 timed out on 27 March 2025 without a decision deposit, so it was never voted on. We built it anyway. Most of what this paper offers was first set out there: the collective on Kreivo, membership as the way in, and a Kusama that is more than Polkadot's test network.

**Who we are.** [Birdbrain](https://birdbrain.wtf) is the open-source lab of [Decent Partners](https://www.decent.partners), and a collective on Kreivo (community 1786). Since January 2026 we have built in public, on Kusama mainnet:

**Collectives, not only us.** We are beginning to onboard other collectives and their members onto the same infrastructure, each with its own community on Kreivo, its own keys and its own records on Kusama. The first is [Four To The Floor](https://fttf.decent.partners), the music film series that began on Channel 4 in 2014 and is becoming a platform its artists and audience join and own. It runs on the systems this paper describes: passkey sign-in and membership on Kreivo, with credits for the work designed to go on chain once the person credited confirms them. It is built to be a home for arts and culture well beyond the UK. Decent Partners is incubating it and is beginning to sign artists to new deals built around what this infrastructure can do. Decent Partners is backed by well-known figures in UK music, among them Matt Black, co-founder of Ninja Tune. That is the kind of demand an independent Kusama is for: groups of real people, outside the crypto industry, choosing to live on the network.

**One tool for the people who run the network.** Murmur is the open app underneath, and the Birdbrain app is its edition for this collective. We are building it into one place where Kusama's operators and contributors sign in with their own key, meet, talk, keep their notes and records, pay and get paid on Asset Hub, and take part in the steward's decisions. One app holds several collectives, so a validator's own group and Birdbrain sit side by side in the same window. The validator dashboard, the cost survey and the steward's proposals are meant to live there too. Those parts are not built yet.

**What we cannot yet do.** Birdbrain cannot maintain Kusama's runtime alone today. Nothing we have built is audited, and Jambo is lab code. The steward needs, at minimum, two independent JAM client teams, experienced Kusama validators and runtime engineers. This referendum is how we ask them to join, and how KSM holders tell them there is a mandate worth joining.

### The ambition: a steward KSM holders can delegate to

A Wish for Change records what KSM holders want. It does not enact it. On Kusama, enactment follows stake-weighted votes, and OpenGov lets any holder delegate their vote, track by track, to anyone they trust, keeping their KSM and taking the delegation back at any time.

So the path is plain. When Birdbrain's membership is a credible association of infrastructure partners, able to steward an independent Kusama, and holds a plan that is fully scoped, specified and costed, KSM holders with a material interest in a different path can delegate their votes to it. Enough delegated stake would let that plan be carried through Kusama's governance and enacted, not only wished for. That is the ambition, stated openly: an alternative that can win a stake-weighted vote, and with it a continuation of Kusama's chaos.

Delegation should follow the work, not come before it. We are not asking for it now. Before Birdbrain accepts any delegated vote, its members will set and publish how those votes are cast, and every vote it casts will be published with its reasons.

KSM DAO can withdraw this mandate by referendum at any point. Any funding is a separate proposal against these milestones.

## 8. What this option does not solve

- **JAM has not shipped.** No JAM network runs in production. Until one does, Kusama runs today's relay chain with its current validators, paid as now.

- **Moving KSM balances and parachains to JAM** today depends on software only Parity builds. The transition plan has to answer this.

- **Nobody yet knows whether commission at #573's 2.4% issuance covers the network.** That is what the costing answers. If it does not, revenue from use has to close the gap, and there is little today.

It asks KSM holders to compare two directions, the one they voted for and the one now proposed, and to choose between them in the open.
